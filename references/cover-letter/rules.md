# Cover Letter Rules

This file defines reusable writing rules for Easy Apply cover letters. Candidate facts are governed by `../common/grounding.md`; the drafting sequence is defined in `workflow.md`.

---

## 1. Purpose

A cover letter is not a prose copy of the resume.

Its job is to make a concise, evidence-based case for two questions:

```text
Why this candidate?
Why this role/employer?
```

Use the resume for breadth and the cover letter for selection, context, motivation, and connection.

---

## 2. Grounding

Every factual candidate claim must be supported by:

- `profile.md`; or
- explicit current-session user information for this application.

Never invent or upgrade facts in order to create a stronger story.

Do not fabricate:

```text
personal interactions with the company
customer incidents
production incidents
leadership scope
metrics/results
technologies used
certifications
work-rights facts
motivation or emotional attachment
```

If the best story is uncertain, ask the user rather than filling the gap.

---

## 3. Specificity without invention

A strong letter should feel specific to the role, but specificity must come from real evidence.

Good specificity includes:

- naming the role accurately;
- referring to responsibilities or problems actually described in the JD;
- selecting candidate experiences that genuinely connect to those responsibilities;
- referring to company/product/domain facts only when known from reliable current material;
- explaining why an existing candidate experience makes the role meaningful.

Avoid generic praise such as:

```text
I have always admired your prestigious company.
Your innovative culture perfectly aligns with my values.
I am passionate about excellence and collaboration.
```

unless the user has supplied concrete evidence or a real reason behind the statement.

---

## 4. Select, do not summarize

Do not attempt to mention every job, project, skill, or qualification.

Choose the strongest few pieces of evidence and explain the connection.

Prefer:

```text
experience -> capability learned -> relevance to this role
```

over:

```text
resume bullet -> same bullet rewritten as a sentence
```

The letter should add interpretation that the resume cannot show as efficiently.

---

## 5. Personal story

A personal story can strengthen a letter when it is real and relevant.

Rules:

- ask the user whether one exists before drafting;
- do not assume a previous employer/company story transfers to another application;
- do not create a fictional anecdote because the letter feels generic;
- if no story exists, write a strong evidence-based letter without one.

A story is optional. Factual accuracy is not.

---

## 6. Structure

Use a short professional structure appropriate to the user's writing preferences and the application context.

A common pattern is:

```text
opening / role + reason for interest
relevant evidence
additional evidence or employer connection
why this employer/role
close
```

This is not a mandatory paragraph count.

Avoid formulaic repetition across applications. The strongest evidence should determine the structure, not a rigid template.

---

## 7. Style

Default style:

- concise;
- plain professional language;
- specific rather than decorative;
- short-to-medium sentences;
- confident without exaggeration;
- natural transitions;
- minimal jargon unless the role requires it.

Persistent style preferences explicitly confirmed by the user belong in `easy-apply.yaml` under writing preferences rather than being hard-coded here.

Do not impose one user's punctuation, paragraph-length, salutation, signature, or tone preferences on every Skill user.

---

## 8. Do not foreground gaps unnecessarily

A cover letter is not a deficiency report.

Do not add a paragraph listing technologies, years of experience, or qualifications the candidate lacks unless:

- the application explicitly asks the candidate to address them; or
- the user wants to explain a material gap.

Do not conceal a direct application question or make a false claim. The rule is simply not to volunteer irrelevant negatives.

---

## 9. Addressee and company accuracy

Use a named addressee only when the identity is reliable and relevant to the application.

Otherwise use an appropriate generic professional salutation.

Do not infer a hiring manager's name from unrelated staff, product teams, department names, or ambiguous JD text.

Make sure:

```text
company name
role title
team/product reference
location when mentioned
```

match the current authoritative job information.

---

## 10. Conflicting job information

If a job board, recruiter page, employer page, or ATS disagrees materially about the role:

- prefer the most authoritative current source for drafting;
- do not silently merge conflicting facts;
- tell the user about material conflicts that could affect the application.

Examples include different location, seniority, job title, experience requirement, or application status.

---

## 11. Employer restrictions on AI writing

If the employer explicitly instructs applicants not to use AI-generated writing:

- do not ghostwrite the cover letter;
- do not provide a disguised ready-to-submit draft;
- the Skill may help interpret the prompt, ask planning questions, organize the user's own ideas, or proofread grammar/spelling after the user writes the content.

Follow the employer's instruction rather than trying to work around it.

---

## 12. Final factual audit

Before finalizing, check every substantive candidate claim against the factual source set.

Pay particular attention to claims involving:

```text
ownership
leadership
production responsibility
customer impact
metrics
technical tools
industry experience
motivation/company interaction
```

If a sentence cannot be grounded, revise or remove it.

The final letter should be stronger because it selects and explains real evidence, not because it invents stronger evidence.
